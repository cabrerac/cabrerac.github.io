## Conclusions

<div class="rows" style="height: 100%">
    <div class="row" style="height: 100%">
        <div class="columns" style="width: 100%">
            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 100%">
                <ul>
                    <li><b>Multi-agent systems are AI systems.</b> They inherit the same intellectual debt: opaque decisions, untraceable interactions, and emergent behaviour that is hard to explain.</li>
                    <br>
                    <li><b>DOAgent</b> addresses this through <b>data-oriented principles</b>: a shared data model where agent decisions, outcomes, and traces are observable, queryable, and analysable — regardless of the policy type.</li>
                    <br>
                    <li><b>LLMs contribute observable reasoning</b> to multi-agent systems. Policy factorisation — separating reasoning (Z) from action (A) — becomes natural with LLMs. But the coordination architecture, topology, and provenance patterns are established MAS foundations.</li>
                    <br>
                    <li><b>The "I don't know" function</b> emerges from reasoning. When agents can explain why they abstain, the system becomes more trustworthy — and the intellectual debt decreases.</li>
                </ul>
            </div>
        </div>
    </div>
</div>
