                <p><b>Map of the debate (Mittelstadt et al.)</b></p>
                <ul>
                    <li><b>Discrimination</b> and unfair treatment of groups</li>
                    <li><b>Autonomy</b> and loss of meaningful choice</li>
                    <li><b>Non-transparency</b> and opaque decisions</li>
                    <li><b>Privacy</b> and unwanted exposure</li>
                    <li>Plus procedural, psychological, and economic harms</li>
                </ul>

                <p><b>Privacy</b></p>
                <p>Control over appropriate flows of personal information in context. Not only secrecy, but who receives what, when, and for what purpose.</p>
                <p><a href="https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3885612" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Nissenbaum, 2004)</a> · UDHR Art. 12 via <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02667" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Prabhakaran et al., 2022)</a></p>

## Ethical Requirements

<div class="rows" style="height: 100%">
    <div class="row" style="height: 100%">
        <div class="columns" style="width: 100%">

            </div>
            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 50%">
                <p><b>Fairness</b></p>
                <p>Just and equitable treatment across groups. In algorithms, asks whether similar people are treated similarly and whether historical bias is reproduced.</p>
                <p>Linked to non-discrimination (UDHR) and justice in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/2053951716679679" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Mittelstadt et al., 2016)</a></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </div>

                <p><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02667" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Prabhakaran et al., 2022)</a> · governance duties in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/1795194.1795237" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Khatri &amp; Brown, 2010)</a></p>

            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 50%">

                <p>Normative study of what we ought to do when data and algorithms affect people. Goes beyond compliance to ask what harm we accept or refuse.</p>
                <p>Assigned anchor: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/2053951716679679" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Mittelstadt et al., 2016)</a></p>

## Ethical Requirements

    <div class="row" style="height: 100%">

            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 50%">
                <p><b>Transparency</b></p>
                <p>Making methods, data limits, and uncertainty visible to stakeholders. Includes intelligibility: can an affected person or auditor understand what was done?</p>
                <p>Harm type «non-transparency» in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/2053951716679679" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Mittelstadt et al., 2016)</a></p>
            </div>
