## Ethical Requirements

<div class="rows" style="height: 100%">
    <div class="row" style="height: 100%">
        <div class="columns" style="width: 100%">
            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 50%">
                <p><b>First-order challenges — two types</b></p>
                <p><a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-023-00309-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Afroogh et al., 2024</a> split value challenges facing AI systems:</p>
                <ul>
                    <li><b>Ethical–legal:</b> fairness, privacy, accountability — moral and legal rights and duties</li>
                    <li><b>Multi-dimensional:</b> trust, transparency, explainability — social acceptance, intelligibility, and confidence in the system</li>
                </ul>
                <p>Both types are first-order: they shape how data projects affect people directly.</p>
            </div>

        <div class="columns" style="width: 100%">
            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 50%">

            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 50%">
                <p><b>Trade-offs as decisions are not easy</b></p>
                <p>First-order values can pull in opposite directions. For example when responding to a disaster: tracing phones may support <b>fair</b> rescue reach but challenge <b>privacy</b>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-023-00309-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Afroogh et al., 2024)</a></p>
                <br>
                <p>Public-sector guidance asks teams to document tensions, consult human-rights frameworks, and revisit choices as the project evolves. <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-ethics-framework/data-and-ai-ethics-framework" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(UK Data and AI Ethics Framework)</a></p>
            </div>

<div class="rows" style="height: 100%">
    <div class="row" style="height: 100%">
        <div class="columns" style="width: 100%">

            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 50%">
                <p><b>Privacy</b> <span style="font-weight: normal">(ethical–legal)</span></p>
                <p>Control over appropriate flows of personal information in context. Not only secrecy, but who receives what, when, and for what purpose.</p>
                <p><a href="https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3885612" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Nissenbaum, 2004)</a></p>
                <p>UDHR Art. 12 <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02667" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Prabhakaran et al., 2022)</a></p>
            </div>

## Ethical Requirements

<div class="rows" style="height: 100%">
    <div class="row" style="height: 100%">
        <div class="columns" style="width: 100%">
            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 50%">
                <img class="external-svg" src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0e/Scale_of_justice_2.svg" alt="Fairness" style="height: 280px">
            </div>
            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 50%">
                <p><b>Fairness</b> <span style="font-weight: normal">(ethical–legal)</span></p>
                <p>Just and equitable treatment across groups. In algorithms, asks whether similar people are treated similarly and whether historical bias is reproduced.</p>
                <p>Linked to non-discrimination (UDHR) and justice in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/2053951716679679" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Mittelstadt et al., 2016)</a></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </div>

    <div class="row" style="height: 100%">

            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 50%">
                <p><b>Accountability</b> <span style="font-weight: normal">(ethical–legal)</span></p>
                <p>Clear answer to "who is responsible for the way the system works and for harm it causes?" Includes documentation, audit trails, and remedial action.</p>
                <p><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02667" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Prabhakaran et al., 2022)</a></p>

                <p><b>Trust</b> <span style="font-weight: normal">(multi-dimensional)</span></p>
                <p>Confidence that a data system is competent, honest, and used for legitimate purposes. Distrust grows with opaque systems, security failures, and unfair outcomes. Linked to privacy and fairness in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-023-00309-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Afroogh et al., 2024)</a></p>

## Ethical Requirements

<div class="rows" style="height: 100%">
    <div class="row" style="height: 100%">
        <div class="columns" style="width: 100%">
            <div class="column vertical-middle text-left" style="width: 50%">
                <p><b>Transparency</b> <span style="font-weight: normal">(multi-dimensional)</span></p>
                <p>Making methods, data limits, and uncertainty visible to stakeholders. <b>Can support accountability, but full openness may conflict with privacy.</b></p>
                <p>Harm type «non-transparency» in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/2053951716679679" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Mittelstadt et al., 2016)</a></p>

                <p><b>Explainability</b> <span style="font-weight: normal">(multi-dimensional)</span></p>
                <p>Ability to describe how a system reached an output. Needed when «black box» systems affect high-stake decisions and when auditors or affected people must understand limits.</p>
                <p>Closely tied to transparency and accountability in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-023-00309-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(Afroogh et al., 2024)</a></p>
